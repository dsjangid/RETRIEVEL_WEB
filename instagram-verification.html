What is Instagram verification service?

The blue checkmark badge that appears next to your username on Instagram will indicate that you have successfully completed the verification process on the platform. When other users see the verification badge, they are aware that the account legitimately belongs to the individual, organization, or company that it asserts to be associated with.

In order to demonstrate that the account has been validated, a blue checkmark will appear next to the name of the account it belongs to. Ever since August 2018, users have been able to submit requests to have their Instagram accounts verified. It is Instagram's official confirmation that the profile in question does, in fact, belong to the famous person, and the sticker serves as that confirmation. When someone makes a request for this, Instagram verification service takes into consideration a number of factors, one of the most important of which is whether or not someone would want to impersonate this account. Before requesting the blue checkmark, you should first demonstrate that you are a well-known individual, a well-known brand, or an influential person.

Just "notable and unique" people or companies could initially be verified. That coveted blue checkmark was usually only awarded to well-known individuals and companies, and the verification procedure was all very mysterious. Meta Verified now offers users the choice to pay for verification as well. Anyone can thus get a verified badge on Facebook or Instagram

The benefits of both verification methods are same:

  • Authenticity: Since the verification procedure calls for a government ID, having a verified badge verifies the legitimacy of your account and informs the community it is the real you.

  • Safety: With the proactive account monitoring from Meta Verified, shield your account from hackers and impersonators.


  • Appearance: When someone searches for a company's name, verified accounts are shown prominently in web comments and search results. They're also a recommended business to follow in the Feed.

Features like special stickers on Instagram Stories and Reels allow verified accounts to stand out with their content. Whatever the verification approach an account used, the blue checkmark looks the same. This complicates determining whether a user paid for the Meta Verified service or obtained it through more traditional channels.

Who meets the criteria to be verified on Instagram?

Anyone, any brand, or any organization can apply to be verified on Instagram. People can now pay Meta and go through a quick process to get their badge. The old rules still apply to brands and groups. This means you have to show Instagram that your account meets these requirements:

In line with Instagram's Community Guidelines and Terms of Use, your account must follow them. Real: Your account must be for a real person, business, or group; it can't be a fake or fan account.

  • Unique: Your account must be the only one that represents a certain person or brand. You may be able to have more than one account for different languages, though.

  • Complete: You must have posted something on the account and have a full bio with a profile picture. Public: If you want to be verified, you have to make your account public.

  • Important: People should search for you or your brand a lot, and news sources should talk about you or your brand.

You don't need a certain number of followers to be verified on Instagram, so don't be afraid to apply with a new account if you think you meet the requirements listed above.
